# Opening: Prosecution

**1. The validator it relies on throws away the designer's authored attacks.** The method's main safety net is to "reuse the game's existing fairness code." But that code was written for a random scheduler, and its answer to a bad attack is that "Rejected attacks are deferred or skipped." In a hand-authored timeline, a deferred or skipped attack means the game quietly rewrites the designer's chart. The case never says how a rejection becomes an authoring error instead of a runtime fallback. Cost: the designer writes "note 120: X-shape," and the player sees something else or nothing.

**2. Timing by note number is blind authoring.** Attacks are keyed like "note 120 ... / notes 124–130: ring combo," across a chart of "~321 notes" in which "the boss's defeat lands on a fixed late note (note 304) in the newest build." Nothing in the record maps note numbers to "chorus, fast sections, finale," and nothing lets the designer see or hear where they are in the song while typing. Cost: slow trial-and-error, and every chart edit shifts the numbers under the designer's attacks.

**3. Bot clear rates cannot judge a fixed chart.** The validator reports "clear rate and action mix" from the bot simulation. The recommended direction, a "fully fixed timeline," is meant for "memorize-and-improve," but the record gives no sign that the bots memorize anything. Also, "No human playtest has been done yet." Cost: numbers that look like balance evidence but measure the wrong player.

**4. It discards the balance already done, and the key choice is still open.** The case admits the targets "were tuned against the random scheduler; replacing it means re-measuring." Yet the choice between a fixed timeline and section pools is listed under "Choice the user still has to make," and pools mean "more cases to validate." Cost: the validator's scope and all re-tuning depend on a decision that hasn't been made.

**5. It builds on stale code.** "The current working folder is behind: the newest build (battle-6...) is on another branch." The one-beat gap check exists only in the "newest build." A validator that reuses "existing fairness code" from this folder would lack it. Cost: rework and merge conflicts before a single pattern ships.
